Grant Tremblay is an astrophysicist at the [Center for Astrophysics \| Harvard & Smithsonian](https://www.cfa.harvard.edu/). He was previously a [NASA Einstein Fellow](https://www.nasa.gov/mission_pages/chandra/news/2014-einstein-fellows.html) at [Yale University](https://astronomy.yale.edu/), a Fellow in the Directorate for Science at the [European Southern Observatory](https://www.eso.org/public/) (ESO), and an Astronomer at ESO's [Very Large Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_Large_Telescope) in Chile. His doctoral thesis work was conducted at the [Space Telescope Science Institute](http://www.stsci.edu/) (operations center of the *Hubble Space Telescope*), the [Johns Hopkins University](https://www.jhu.edu/), and the [Rochester Institute of Technology](http://www.rit.edu/cos/astrophysics/). He currently serves as External Relations Lead and head of the [*Lynx X-ray Observatory*](https://www.lynxobservatory.org) Science Support Office at the [Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory](https://www.cfa.harvard.edu/), and previously supported flight operations for the [*Chandra X-ray Observatory*](https://chandra.si.edu/) as part of the High Resolution Camera Instrument Principal Investigator Team. As of August 2024, Grant is Senior Vice President of the [American Astronomical Society](https://aas.org), Vice Chair of the [NASA Astrophysics Advisory Committee](https://science.nasa.gov/researchers/nac/science-advisory-committees/apac), and a Lecturer in [Harvard's Department of Astronomy](https://astronomy.fas.harvard.edu/).

Dr. Tremblay's research employs highly multiwavelength techniques, using data from the world's most powerful ground- and space-based observatories to better understand star formation amid energetic feedback from supermassive black holes. His portfolio consists of over [one hundred refereed publications](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-5445-5401) and several million dollars in NASA funding. He is also involved in the development of future space missions, including instrumentation for a notional Europa Lander as well as [*Lynx*](https://www.lynxobservatory.org), a NASA-funded [Large Mission Concept study](https://www.greatobservatories.org) considered by the [2020 Decadal Survey in Astronomy & Astrophysics](https://www.nationalacademies.org/our-work/decadal-survey-on-astronomy-and-astrophysics-2020-astro2020).

Dr. Tremblay is the author of two books for the general public. The first, [*Light from the Void: Twenty Years of Discovery with NASA's Chandra X-ray Observatory*](https://www.smithsonianbooks.com/store/aviation-military-history/light-void-chandra-x-ray-observatory/), celebrates *Chandra*'s two decades of discovery and was published by Smithsonian Books in 2019. The second, [*What Do Black Holes Eat for Dinner?*](https://tumblehomebooks.org/book/what-do-black-holes-eat-for-dinner/), is a children's book published by Tumblehome Learning in 2020. Dr. Tremblay is also a regular main cast member on the Discovery and Science Channel's award-winning documentary series [*How the Universe Works*](https://www.sciencechannel.com/tv-shows/how-the-universe-works/) and [*Space's Deepest Secrets*](https://www.sciencechannel.com/tv-shows/spaces-deepest-secrets/), with additional projects for the Smithsonian Channel, the BBC, and PBS/NOVA.
