About

I study star formation and the dynamics of multiphase gas amid energetic feedback from active galaxies, whose central engines are powered by accreting supermassive black holes.

I'm a Federal Astrophysicist and External Relations Lead at the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory, part of the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian. Previously, I served as Deputy P.I. and Project Scientist for the Chandra X-ray Observatory's High Resolution Camera.

Before joining the Smithsonian, I was a NASA Einstein Fellow at Yale University and a Fellow at the European Southern Observatory (ESO) near Munich. I also served as an Astronomer at ESO's Very Large Telescope in the Atacama Desert of Chile. Earlier in my career, I held positions at the Rochester Institute of Technology, the Space Telescope Science Institute, Johns Hopkins University, and the University of Rochester.

My work is supported by the Smithsonian Institution and NASA through grants from the Hubble Space Telescope, Chandra X-ray Observatory, and Spitzer Space Telescope programs.

Research Highlights

My research uses multiwavelength observations to understand how galaxies form stars and how supermassive black holes shape their environments through energetic feedback.

Galactic Fountains

Using ALMA and the VLT/MUSE instrument, we discovered a galaxy-scale fountain of cold molecular gas driven by a supermassive black hole, published in Nature. This was covered by the Washington Post, BBC, New York Times, Popular Science, and TIME.

Black Hole Feedback

My work on AGN feedback explores how supermassive black holes regulate star formation in massive galaxies through powerful jets and outflows that heat and reshape the surrounding gas.

Gravitational Lensing

Our Hubble discovery of strongly lensed galaxies behind massive clusters was featured by NASA, ESA, NBC News, Forbes, and Astronomy Picture of the Day (APOD).

Rogue Black Holes

We identified a supermassive black hole apparently kicked from its host galaxy by gravitational waves, with coverage in NASA press releases, the Washington Post, and Science magazine.

Outreach & Public Engagement

Astronomy on Tap

I organized the New Haven chapter and work with the Boston team of Astronomy on Tap, bringing accessible astronomy talks to bars, breweries, and other casual venues for the public.

Television & Documentary

I appear as a cast member and science advisor on Space's Deepest Secrets (Science Channel) and How the Universe Works (Discovery Channel). My IMDB page has the full list.

Public Events

I love connecting science with popular culture. At Awesome Con in Washington, DC, I moderated a Q&A with David Tennant before an audience of about 5,000 people.

The Banneker Institute

I serve as a research adviser and course instructor for Harvard's Banneker Institute, which prepares undergraduate students of color for graduate programs in astronomy through research, community building, and social justice education.
