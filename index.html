Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian

Grant Tremblay

Astrophysicist studying how galaxies form stars and how supermassive black holes shape the Universe around them.

About

I study star formation and the dynamics of multiphase gas amid energetic feedback from active galaxies, whose central engines are powered by accreting supermassive black holes.

I'm a Federal Astrophysicist and External Relations Lead at the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory, part of the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian. Previously, I was Deputy P.I. and Project Scientist for the Chandra X-ray Observatory's High Resolution Camera.

Before the Smithsonian, I was a NASA Einstein Fellow at Yale University, a Fellow in the Directorate for Science at the European Southern Observatory (ESO) near Munich, and an Astronomer at ESO's Very Large Telescope in the Atacama Desert of Chile. Earlier, I held positions at the Rochester Institute of Technology, the Space Telescope Science Institute, Johns Hopkins University, and the University of Rochester.

As of August 2024, I also serve as Senior Vice President of the American Astronomical Society, Vice Chair of the NASA Astrophysics Advisory Committee, and a Lecturer in Harvard's Department of Astronomy.

Research

My group uses the world's most powerful ground- and space-based observatories — among them ALMA, the VLT/MUSE, Hubble, and Chandra — to understand how galaxies grow their stars, and how the supermassive black holes at their hearts regulate that growth through spectacular, energetic feedback.

Galactic Fountains

With ALMA and the VLT/MUSE, we discovered a galaxy-scale fountain of cold molecular gas lifted and recycled by a supermassive black hole — published in Nature and covered by the Washington Post, BBC, New York Times, and TIME.

Black Hole Feedback

I study how supermassive black holes regulate star formation in massive galaxies through powerful jets and outflows that heat, stir, and reshape the surrounding multiphase gas.

Gravitational Lensing

Our Hubble discovery of strongly lensed "beads on a string" of young stars behind a massive cluster was featured by NASA, ESA, NBC News, Forbes, and Astronomy Picture of the Day.

Recoiling Black Holes

We found evidence for a supermassive black hole apparently kicked from its host galaxy's core by gravitational waves, with coverage from NASA, the Washington Post, and Science.

Education & Outreach

The Banneker & Aztlán Institute

I'm a research adviser and course instructor for Harvard's Banneker & Aztlán Institute, which prepares undergraduate students of color for graduate programs in astronomy through research, community building, and social-justice education. See the 2018 research project →

Astronomy on Tap

I organized the New Haven chapter and work with the Boston team of Astronomy on Tap, bringing accessible astronomy talks to bars, breweries, and other casual venues for the public.

Documentary & Film

I'm a main cast member and science advisor on the Discovery and Science Channel series How the Universe Works and Space's Deepest Secrets, with forthcoming work for the Smithsonian Channel, the BBC, and PBS/NOVA. My IMDb page has the full list.

Public Events

I love connecting science with popular culture. At Awesome Con in Washington, DC, I moderated a Q&A with David Tennant (Doctor Who) before an audience of about 5,000 people.

Code & Software
